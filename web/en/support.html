Support

Answers to common questions about Kolorist – and how to get in touch if you can’t find what you’re looking for.

Contact

  • your device (iPhone, iPad or Mac) and iOS, iPadOS or macOS version
  • the version of Kolorist
  • what you did, what you expected and what happened
  • a screenshot or the colour values involved, if possible

Frequently asked questions

Which colour spaces and colour models are supported?

You can edit colours in OKLCH, OKLab, CIE LCH, CIELab, HSB, HSL, RGB and CMYK. RGB colours can be in Display P3, beyond sRGB. You can also view and edit the values in any ICC profile – built in or imported.

CIELab and CIE LCH use the D50 white point, like ICC profiles, Photoshop and CSS lab(), so the values match your design tools.

The colour value field also understands CSS text: hex, rgb(), hsl(), hwb(), lab(), lch(), oklab(), oklch(), color(), cmyk() and CSS colour names. If you type a description, such as “deep ocean blue” or “dusty pink”, the colour is made in OKLCH and shown in Studio.

When the colour model is CMYK and you show a CMYK profile (or RGB and an RGB profile), the values are entered directly in the profile. The colour is then rendered as it will be in that colour space, without a gamut warning.

What is the difference between Display P3 and sRGB?

Display P3 is a wider gamut than sRGB. The screens on recent iPhone, iPad and Mac models can show P3, and saturated reds, oranges and greens in particular can fall outside what sRGB can hold. Much of the web, older software and many workflows still use sRGB.

Kolorist shows the colour in Display P3 side by side with another colour space – sRGB, Adobe RGB, CMYK or an installed ICC profile – and warns you when the colour is outside that space. When a colour has to fit into a smaller space, its saturation (chroma) is reduced while lightness and hue are preserved, rather than clipping the values. You can also choose to limit new colours to a specific profile.

How do I import an ICC profile?

Use the import option in the app’s ICC profile section and choose an .icc or .icm file. The profile’s name is read from the file itself.

If you’re signed in to iCloud with iCloud Drive turned on, imported profiles are stored in iCloud Drive › Kolorist › Profiler. The folder is visible in Files (iPhone and iPad) and Finder (Mac), and the profiles become available on your other devices. You can also put .icc files directly into that folder. Without iCloud Drive, profiles are stored on the device only.

When converting between profiles you choose a rendering intent (perceptual, relative colorimetric, saturation or absolute colorimetric), and you can compare the results of all four.

How does iCloud sync work?

Palettes and saved colours sync through your private iCloud database when you’re signed in to iCloud with the same Apple Account on all your devices. Only you can access this data – it is not stored with the developer.

If something doesn’t show up on another device:

  • Check that you’re signed in to iCloud with the same Apple Account on both devices.
  • Check that iCloud is turned on for Kolorist in your iCloud settings, and that iCloud Drive is on (for ICC profiles).
  • Give it a little time and a network connection – syncing happens in the background and can take a few minutes.

Without iCloud the app works as usual, but your data is stored on the device only.

Why are the AI features unavailable or simpler than expected?

The AI features use Apple Intelligence and the on-device language model. They require

  • an iPhone, iPad or Mac that supports Apple Intelligence,
  • Apple Intelligence turned on in Settings (System Settings on Mac),
  • the model to have finished downloading, and
  • Apple Intelligence to be available for the language and region you use.

Palettes from value words are grounded in a knowledge base of more than a hundred colour concepts – nature, materials, styles and values – in Norwegian and English. The language model is given the relevant concepts and chooses hue family, lightness and saturation; the colours themselves are computed in OKLCH. The app shows which concepts a suggestion is based on.

When Apple Intelligence isn’t available, the app builds the palette directly from the knowledge base. It works offline, but is less flexible than the language model – for example, free-text adjustments, naming and palette critique may be limited or unavailable.

Either way, everything happens on device. What you type is not sent to the developer.

The app can’t use the camera. What do I do?

The first time you open the camera, the system asks for permission. If you declined, you can change it here:

  • iPhone and iPad: Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera › turn on Kolorist.
  • Mac: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera › turn on Kolorist.

The camera is only used live on the device to pick colours and for the colour vision filter. No images are stored or sent.

You can also pick colours from images you choose yourself, without camera access.

Can I pick colours from other apps?

On Mac: Yes, the screen eyedropper picks colours from anywhere on screen.

On iPhone and iPad: The system doesn’t let apps read the screen outside themselves. Take a screenshot and pick the colour from the image in Kolorist instead.

What does “Copy to …” do?

Touch and hold a colour or a palette (right-click on Mac) and choose Copy to and the app you’ll paste into. Kolorist puts the colours on the clipboard in the format that app accepts:

  • Figma, Sketch and Affinity: filled shapes (SVG) you paste straight onto the canvas.
  • Illustrator and InDesign: vector shapes (PDF) with colour space and ICC profile – colours saved in a CMYK profile arrive as CMYK.
  • Photoshop: shape layers (PDF, with an image as fallback), and the hex value for the colour picker.
  • Pages, Keynote and Numbers: swatches as PDF and image, and on Mac also as a system colour.
  • CSS and SwiftUI: code as text.

With Universal Clipboard you can copy on iPhone or iPad and paste on Mac. On Mac you can also drag swatches straight into other apps.

How does the app simulate colour vision deficiency?

Under Assess › Colour vision you see the palette with protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia and achromatopsia. Severity can be set below 100 % for partial deficiencies, which are the most common. The app shows which colour pairs become hard to tell apart (ΔE2000), and the camera button next to each deficiency shows your surroundings through the same filter.

The simulation is based on the model by Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009). It is an approximation – how colours are perceived varies between people. Read more about the methods.

How do I get my colours into Adobe apps?
  • Adobe ASE (Adobe Swatch Exchange) can be imported as swatches in Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign and others.
  • Photoshop ACO is Photoshop’s own swatch format, with colour names. Colours outside sRGB are stored as Lab so they aren’t clipped.

Export from the palette, save the file in Files or Finder, and open it from the Swatches panel in the Adobe app. For one colour at a time, Copy to is quicker.

How do I get my colours into Figma?
  • Figma variables: Export as DTCG JSON and use Figma’s variable import.
  • Tokens Studio: Export in the Tokens Studio format and load it in the plugin.
  • SVG swatches: Copy and paste into Figma – the colours arrive as filled shapes.

Other formats: CSS, W3C Design Tokens, GIMP GPL, SwiftUI and hex list.

Which methods and sources does the app build on?

Every section in the app has a “Method: …” line. Tap it to see the explanation and source. All of them are collected at the bottom of Palettes under Methods and sources, and on the Methods and sources page.

How do I use Siri and Shortcuts?

Kolorist provides actions in the Shortcuts app: create a palette from value words, create a gradient, convert a colour, check contrast and describe a colour. Use them in your own shortcuts or ask Siri.
