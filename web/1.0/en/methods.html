Methods and sources

Kolorist builds on established colour science and open standards. This page lists what the app uses, where it comes from, and what was developed for the app. The same explanations are in the app: tap “Method: …” below a section, or choose Methods and sources at the bottom of Palettes.

OKLab and OKLCH

A perceptual colour space where equal numeric steps are perceived as equally large colour differences. Kolorist computes gradients in OKLab, and tone scales, lighter/darker steps, harmonies and saturation in OKLCH (lightness, chroma, hue).

Source: Björn Ottosson: “A perceptual color space for image processing” (2020)

CSS Color Module Level 4

The standard for colour on the web. Kolorist uses the specification’s matrices and transfer functions for sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB, Lab and OKLab, its gamut mapping (chroma is reduced in OKLCH while lightness and hue are preserved) and its colour text syntax.

Source: W3C: CSS Color Module Level 4 (Candidate Recommendation)

CIELab, CIE LCH and Bradford adaptation

The CIE colour space based on human colour vision. Kolorist reports Lab and LCH with a D50 white point, like ICC profiles and Photoshop, and uses the Bradford transform between D65 and D50.

Source: CIE 15:2018 Colorimetry; K. M. Lam (1985), the Bradford transform

CIEDE2000 (ΔE00)

The CIE formula for perceived colour difference. Used when comparing colours, for gamut deviations and in the analysis of colours that become hard to tell apart with colour vision deficiency. The implementation is checked against the test data in Sharma et al.

Source: G. Sharma, W. Wu, E. N. Dalal: Color Research & Application 30(1), 2005; CIE 142-2001

WCAG 2.2 contrast

The W3C requirements for contrast between text/graphics and background, computed from relative luminance. “Fix” changes only lightness (OKLCH) until the requirement is met.

Source: W3C: Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2, success criteria 1.4.3, 1.4.6 and 1.4.11

Colour vision deficiency simulation

A physiologically based model of protan, deutan and tritan deficiencies, with matrices in linear sRGB and severity as a blend with normal vision. Achromatopsia is shown as luminance alone. Used in Assess › Colour vision, the contrast preview and the camera filter. The simulation is an approximation; experience varies between people.

Source: G. M. Machado, M. M. Oliveira, L. A. F. Fernandes: IEEE TVCG 15(6), 2009

ICC profiles and colour management

Conversion between colour spaces via ICC profiles, with the chosen rendering intent. Performed by Apple’s colour management (ColorSync). Profiles are not bundled with the app; Kolorist uses the system’s profiles and profiles you import.

Source: International Colour Consortium (ICC.1); Apple ColorSync via Core Graphics

Clean CMYK values (UCR/GCR)

The common grey component of C, M and Y is moved to black, and Kolorist searches for the separation with the fewest inks that stays within 1 ΔE00 of the profile’s own separation.

Source: Established print technique; the search was developed for Kolorist

Colour wheels and harmonies

Complementary, split-complementary, analogous and even distributions are computed as angles on the chosen colour wheel: OKLCH, CIE LCH, HSL or RYB. The RYB wheel is a piecewise linear mapping to RGB hue made for the app.

Source: Classical colour theory (Johannes Itten for RYB); the mappings were developed for Kolorist

Colour semantics and AI

Palettes from value words and “Describe a colour” build on a knowledge base of colour concepts made for the app. The on-device language model interprets the words and chooses hue families and expression. The palette itself is then composed by fixed rules in OKLCH: harmony principle, equal value or equal saturation, one accent, a light or dark background and text with at least 7:1 contrast. Text about colour meaning describes conventions, not scientific facts.

Source: Developed for Kolorist; language model from Apple (Foundation Models)

About the implementation

The formulas are implemented in the app from the published specifications and papers above, and checked with tests against reference values where available. If you have questions about a method or find something that doesn’t add up, please email eivind.johansen@ntnu.no.
